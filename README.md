# Self-Employed Tax Calculator UK — Income Tax and Class 4 NI

> On £40,000 of profit you pay Income Tax on everything above £12,570, plus Class 4 National Insurance at 6% between £12,570 and £50,270.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

Self-employed National Insurance is different from an employee's.

| | Rate | Band |
|---|---:|---|
| Class 4 | 6% | £12,570 to £50,270 |
| Class 4 | 2% | above £50,270 |
| Class 2 | treated as paid | profits above £7,105 |

Class 2 is no longer charged but is **treated as paid** above £7,105, which protects your State Pension record. Below that you can pay it voluntarily at £3.65 a week to keep the record intact.

There is a trading allowance of £1,000. Below that you need not register or declare at all.

**Payments on account** catch out almost every new sole trader: your first bill can be 150% of the tax due, because you pay last year's tax plus half of next year's in one go.

## What this does not cover

It works on profit, not turnover. What counts as an allowable expense is its own subject, and getting it wrong is the most common reason a return is challenged.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
